AI lead interviews: only what job descriptions ask for

You already know Python and infra, so this skips tutorials. It covers the concepts and tools that show up again and again in AI lead and manager job descriptions, at interview depth, with a 6-week plan of 3 hours on weekdays and 5 on weekends.

1What job descriptions actually ask for

Job-posting analyses agree on the core. In 4,155 AI Engineer postings, LLMs appeared in 55.5%, agents in 47.3%, generative AI in 40.6%, RAG in 39.6%, vector databases in 24.5%, LangChain in 23.4% and MLOps in 20% (InterviewStack). In another set of 390 postings, 56% mentioned AI evaluation, 50% agents and 26% RAG (KDnuggets). LinkedIn lists LangChain, RAG and PyTorch as the most common AI engineer skills (Dice).

2Concepts at interview depth

Each card has the few points interviewers probe and a line you can say out loud. Skip anything not here.

3Tools: one line each

Know what each is for and when you would pick it. Use means do it hands-on in the plan; Know means a one-line explanation is enough.

46-week plan

Weekdays are 3 hours and weekends 5 hours. Suggested split: weekdays for reading and short labs, weekends for the build and review blocks. One small project ties the tools together so you can say "I built this". Select a week to jump to it. Ticks are saved in this browser.

Project sliceWhat you build (small)Week
Runbook assistantRAG over a few runbooks with pgvector, hybrid search, reranking, citations1 to 2
AgentLangGraph agent with 2 tools, human approval before any action, an MCP tool server, traced in LangSmith3
Quality and safety30-question golden set scored with RAGAS or DeepEval in CI, LiteLLM gateway with fallback, input and output guardrails4 to 5
Platform storyOne-page architecture covering serving (vLLM or managed), Kubernetes, cost and security5 to 6

Short on time: skip the A2A reading, the fine-tuning note and one system-design drill. Keep the project and the mock interviews. Each week is 5 weekdays at 3 h plus a weekend of 10 h, shown as 7 tasks.
